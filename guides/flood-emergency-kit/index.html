Flood Emergency Kit: Water-Resistant Supplies for Rising Waters

Build a flood emergency kit with water-resistant supplies, evacuation essentials, and flood-specific safety gear for your family.

Last updated: July 25, 2025By EmergencyKitGuide Editorial Team

Why Flood Emergency Kits Require Waterproof Supplies

A flood emergency kit is fundamentally different from other disaster kits because water itself is the primary threat. Unlike earthquakes, tornadoes, or wildfires, floods inundate your home and belongings with contaminated water that destroys everything it touches. Paper documents disintegrate, electronics short-circuit, clothing and bedding become unsalvageable, and food supplies are contaminated by sewage and chemicals carried in floodwaters. This means every critical item in your flood kit must be protected from water damage using waterproof containers, dry bags, and elevated storage.

Floods are the most common and costly natural disaster in the United States. According to FEMA, just one inch of floodwater can cause over $25,000 in property damage. Flash floods can develop in minutes with little to no warning, and even slow-rising river floods can overtop levees and overwhelm drainage systems. The National Weather Service issues hundreds of flood watches and warnings every year across the country. Having a flood-specific emergency kit with water-resistant supplies means that when water starts rising, you can grab your kit and evacuate with your most critical belongings intact. Waterproof Bags and Container Strategy

The foundation of any flood emergency kit is a layered waterproofing system that protects your most critical supplies. Heavy-duty dry bags with roll-top closures are the gold standard for waterproof storage. These bags, originally designed for kayaking and whitewater rafting, create a watertight seal when the top is rolled down and buckled. Available in sizes ranging from 5 liters to over 100 liters, they accommodate everything from small document pouches to full changes of clothing. Vacuum-sealed bags provide an additional layer of protection for documents and electronics stored inside dry bags.

Use a tiered approach: place your most critical documents including identification, insurance policies, and bank information in a waterproof document sleeve, then inside a small dry bag, then inside a hard-sided waterproof case. For electronics like phones and power banks, use IPX7-rated waterproof cases or sealed plastic pouches. Store your entire flood kit in a large waterproof duffel bag or dry bag that can be grabbed and carried out the door in seconds. Elevate your flood kit storage on a shelf or upper floor, never on the ground floor or in a basement where floodwater will reach first. For more on waterproof storage strategies, see our 72-hour kit guide.

Elevated Document Storage and Important Copies

Protecting important documents is one of the highest priorities in flood preparedness. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals, and debris that can destroy paper documents within hours. Store photocopies of all critical documents in a waterproof fireproof safe placed on the highest accessible floor of your home. Include photo IDs, Social Security cards, birth certificates, passports, insurance policies, vehicle titles, mortgage documents, bank statements, medical records, and prescription information. Keep a complete set of these copies in your flood evacuation kit as well.

Digital backups are equally important. Scan every critical document and store the files on an encrypted USB drive kept in a waterproof case. Upload copies to a secure cloud storage service like Google Drive or Dropbox so they are accessible from any device with internet access. Take photographs of every room in your home and all valuable belongings for insurance documentation purposes. Store these photographs in the same cloud account. Before flood season begins, verify that your insurance policy explicitly covers flood damage, as standard homeowner's insurance typically does not. Wading Boots, Life Jackets, and Water Safety Gear

Floods create immediate water safety hazards that no other disaster type presents. Even shallow moving water can knock an adult off their feet, and contaminated floodwater poses serious health risks through skin contact and ingestion. Include a pair of waterproof wading boots or hip waders in your flood emergency kit for navigating flooded streets and yards. Sturdy wading boots with ankle support and puncture-resistant soles protect your feet from hidden debris including broken glass, nails, and sharp metal submerged in murky water.

If you live in a flood-prone area, especially near rivers or in low-lying coastal regions, consider keeping personal flotation devices (PFDs) for every family member in your flood kit. A lightweight inflatable PFD takes up minimal space and can be donned in seconds if water rises unexpectedly. A rope throw bag allows you to help someone who has been swept off their feet without entering the water yourself. Never walk or drive through moving floodwater. Just six inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult down, and two feet of water can sweep away most vehicles including SUVs. Water Purification After a Flood

One of the cruelest ironies of flooding is that the water surrounding you is undrinkable. Floodwater is contaminated with sewage, agricultural chemicals, petroleum products, industrial waste, and pathogenic bacteria. Even after floodwaters recede, municipal water supplies may remain contaminated for days or weeks. Your flood emergency kit must include both stored drinking water and water purification capabilities. Store at least one gallon of commercially bottled water per person per day for a minimum of three days, stored in your waterproof kit at an elevated location.

For additional purification capacity, include water purification tablets such as Aquatabs or Katadyn Micropur, a portable water filter rated for biological contaminants like the Sawyer Squeeze or LifeStraw, and a small metal pot for boiling water over a portable stove or campfire. Boiling water for at least one minute at a rolling boil kills most pathogens. In an emergency where no other option exists, a solar still can extract small amounts of purified water from contaminated sources. Never use floodwater for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, or washing dishes until it has been properly purified. NOAA Weather Radio and Evacuation Timing

A battery-powered or hand-crank NOAA weather radio is essential for flood preparedness because floods often develop rapidly, particularly flash floods. The National Weather Service issues flash flood watches when conditions are favorable and flash flood warnings when flooding is imminent or occurring. A NOAA radio with Specific Area Message Encoding (SAME) technology alerts you automatically for your specific county, providing critical minutes of lead time that can mean the difference between safe evacuation and being trapped by rising water.

Timing your evacuation during a flood is critical. Never wait until you can see water on your street. Flash floods can develop in minutes, and even slow-rising floods can trap you in your home once water blocks your exits. Evacuate as soon as a flash flood warning is issued for your area or when you observe water rising rapidly in nearby streams, creeks, or storm drains. Memorize at least two evacuation routes that lead to higher ground, and drive them before flood season so you know the turns and landmarks. Keep your vehicle's gas tank at least three-quarters full during flood season and park on higher ground when floods are forecast. For communication strategies, see our disaster preparedness plan.

Vehicle Flood Preparation

Your vehicle is a critical evacuation tool during floods, but it is also highly vulnerable to flood damage. Modern vehicles contain extensive electronic systems that are destroyed by water immersion. Even a few inches of water can total a vehicle by corroding wiring harnesses, shorting out computer modules, and contaminating the transmission and differential fluids. During flood season, park your vehicle on the highest available ground, ideally in a garage on an elevated floor or on higher terrain away from flood-prone areas.

Prepare a vehicle-specific flood emergency kit that includes a window breaker tool, a seatbelt cutter, reflective triangles, jumper cables, a portable air compressor, a basic tool kit, and a waterproof bag containing copies of your vehicle registration and insurance. Keep a change of clothes and sturdy shoes in your vehicle during flood season. Know the depth of every underpass and low-lying road on your evacuation route. If you encounter standing water on the road, turn around and find an alternate route. Never attempt to drive through water of unknown depth. First Aid for Flood-Specific Injuries

Flood injuries differ from other disaster injuries because they often involve waterborne contaminants that dramatically increase infection risk. Cuts and puncture wounds sustained in floodwater are extremely prone to infection from bacteria including Vibrio vulnificus, a potentially deadly flesh-eating bacteria found in warm coastal waters and increasingly in inland floodwater. Your flood emergency first aid kit must include wound cleaning supplies, antibiotic ointment, and a supply of oral antibiotics if prescribed by your physician.

Stock your flood-specific first aid kit with adhesive bandages, sterile gauze pads, medical tape, butterfly closure strips, antiseptic wipes, hydrogen peroxide, burn cream, pain relievers, antihistamines, anti-diarrheal medication, and a tetanus booster record. Wear waterproof gloves when handling floodwater or debris. Wash any wound exposed to floodwater immediately with clean water and apply antibiotic ointment. Seek medical attention for any wound that shows signs of infection including redness, swelling, warmth, red streaking, or pus. Include N95 masks in your kit for protection against mold spores during post-flood cleanup. For comprehensive first aid guidance, see our 72-hour kit guide.

Post-Flood Mold Prevention Supplies

Mold begins growing on wet surfaces within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure, making it one of the most immediate and persistent threats after a flood. Mold spores cause respiratory problems, allergic reactions, and worsening of asthma symptoms, and certain types like black mold produce mycotoxins that pose serious long-term health risks. Your flood emergency kit should include post-flood mold prevention supplies so you can begin remediation immediately after waters recede. Pack rubber gloves, N95 respirator masks, safety goggles, and a supply of bleach for making a sanitizing solution of one cup bleach per gallon of water.

Include heavy-duty garbage bags for removing wet carpet, padding, drywall, and other porous materials that cannot be salvaged. A wet-dry vacuum is invaluable for removing standing water from your home. Portable dehumidifiers and high-velocity fans speed the drying process dramatically. Mold-inhibiting primer can be applied to cleaned walls before repainting to prevent regrowth. Remove all furniture, boxes, and belongings from wet areas and dry them outdoors in direct sunlight whenever possible. The faster you can dry your home after a flood, the less mold damage you will experience. For additional home safety preparation, consult our home emergency kit guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of waterproof bags should I use for my flood kit?
Use heavy-duty dry bags with roll-top closures, watertight dry boxes, or vacuum-sealed bags for documents and electronics. Waterproof stuff sacks rated IPX7 or higher provide reliable protection. For critical documents like IDs and insurance policies, place them in a waterproof document sleeve inside a sealed dry bag for double protection. Avoid standard ziplock bags alone as they can fail under pressure.
When should I evacuate during a flood?
Evacuate immediately when authorities issue a flood warning or mandatory evacuation order. Do not wait for water to reach your doorstep. Flash floods can develop in minutes with little warning. Turn around, do not drown: never drive through flooded roadways as just six inches of moving water can knock you down and two feet can sweep away a vehicle.
How do I purify water after a flood?
Flood water is contaminated with sewage chemicals and debris. Use commercially bottled water whenever possible. If you must use local water, boil it for at least one minute at a rolling boil. Water purification tablets, portable filters rated for biological contaminants, and UV sterilization devices are also effective. Never use flood water for drinking cooking or brushing teeth.
How should I prepare my vehicle for flood season?
Keep your gas tank at least three-quarters full during flood season. Park on higher ground when floods are forecast. Move important documents and electronics from your vehicle to higher storage. Ensure your insurance covers flood damage. Consider installing a vehicle emergency kit with a window breaker, seatbelt cutter, and reflective triangles. Never attempt to drive through standing water of unknown depth.
How do I prevent mold after a flood?
Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure. Remove all wet materials including carpet padding and drywall within 48 hours. Clean hard surfaces with a solution of one cup bleach per gallon of water. Run dehumidifiers and fans continuously. Remove furniture and belongings from wet areas. Apply mold-inhibiting primer to cleaned walls before repainting. Wear N95 masks and rubber gloves during cleanup.
